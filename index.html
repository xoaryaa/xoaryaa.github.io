Fault-Tolerant LoRA: Recovering Accuracy under Memory Faults

Oct 2026 – Present

BAAHL (Brooklyn Application, Architecture, and Hardware Lab), Prof. Brandon Reagen

  • Built a SCALE-Sim pipeline to quantify the hardware cost of LoRA-based fault recovery in ResNet-18 (21 base + 42 LoRA GEMMs): rank-4 adapters add 15.3% cycles (1.35M → 1.56M) on a 32×32 weight-stationary array, while clustering plus adapters cuts stored weight bytes 73.7%.
  • Showed the network is memory-bound on a 64×64 weight-stationary array, with stalls at 82–90% of cycles: doubling SRAM (64 → 128 KiB) cut total cycles 42%, while a 4× larger PE array cut compute cycles 51% but total cycles only 9%.
  • Wrote a SCALE-Sim memory-model patch (explicit prefetch cursor, FIFO active-window residency, FP32 capacity accounting) and compared dataflows: WS took 6.2M cycles, OS 9.3M and IS 68.1M at 64 KiB SRAM and 10 words/cycle. Upstreamed a stall-accounting fix (merged PR #169).
  • Prototyped a compressed-weight supply path (DRAM fetch → codebook decode → SRAM staging) with 28 unit tests; offline first-use prefetch ordering cut one layer's cycles 8.0× (an upper bound).

ArchTune: GPU Microarchitecture & GEMM Optimization

2026
  • Built 9 CUDA GEMM kernels (coalescing, shared-memory tiling with compile-time vs. runtime tile sizes, 8×8 register tiling) and measured them on an NVIDIA T4 (N=4096): the register-tiled kernel reached 2.88 TFLOP/s, 73% of cuBLAS (82% at N=2048) and 3.6× faster than shared-memory tiling, though GPGPU-Sim at N=256 ranked it the slowest tiled kernel because the small grid left most SMs idle.
  • Compared simulated and measured trends: on the T4, uncoalesced loads cost 7.5× (4.0× simulated) and runtime tile sizes 16–37% (8–12% simulated), while shared-memory tiling gained only 1.7× (3.2× simulated). Nsight Compute showed full occupancy and zero bank conflicts but a ~75% SM-throughput ceiling, consistent with shared-memory load pressure.
  • Optimized a 512×512 fp32 CPU matmul on Apple M4 with cache tiling to 5.06 ms (≈53 GFLOP/s), timing tile sizes 8–128 (30 runs each) and finding no monotonic trend with tile size.
